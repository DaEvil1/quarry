# Decisions

Dated one-line verdicts. The reasoning lives in the spec and in PR discussions.

- 2026-10-20: Build a custom app instead of adopting Obsidian or Scrivener, so the tool fits the process rather than the other way round.
- 2026-10-20: Postgres is the source of truth; CodeMirror 6 for the editor; FastAPI + Vue 3 PWA; Android phone; passkey login in the app.
- 2026-10-21: Name is Quarry.
- 2026-10-21: No content git repo. Database backups plus "Export now" cover safety; a git mirror may come back later as an option.
- 2026-10-21: A world level sits above projects. The bible belongs to the world; manuscripts belong to projects; items, journal and decisions can be either.
- 2026-10-21: A users table from day one (single user for now), because co-writing is a real possibility.
- 2026-10-21: The code repo is public, so no story content is ever committed; tests use made-up fixtures.
- 2026-10-21: Deploy like Gauntlet (droplet, host nginx, managed Postgres, image from GHCR), not on Komodo.
- 2026-10-21: No importer for the Google Doc or the Obsidian vault. Quarry starts empty; after phase 4 the doc is converted by hand into the export layout and loaded with Restore.
- 2026-10-21: Phase 1 live at quarry.daevil1.com. Passkeys from 1Password don't reach Chrome on Android (Pixel 8, Android 17), so the phone has its own passkey; Quarry needs no change for either.
- 2026-10-21: Capture's entity tag moves to phase 3, where entities exist. Phase 2 links captures to a project or the whole world only.
- 2026-10-21: On the phone, tapping an item opens its actions instead of swipe gestures; every close can be undone.
- 2026-10-21: Offline in phase 2 means new captures and opening the app; the inbox and triage need the network until the local copy in phase 5.
- 2026-10-21: The Journal is built in phase 3. The spec described it but gave it no phase, and the planning essays need a home before the move after phase 4.
- 2026-10-21: Near misses: one edit for words of 5–6 letters, two for 7 or more, with a swapped pair counting as one edit (OSA distance). Two edits on short words flagged too many ordinary words.
- 2026-10-21: Mentions are recomputed on save, and for the whole world when names, aliases or "not a typo" words change; fast enough at a novel's scale, so no background jobs.
- 2026-10-21: Chapters are never deleted once they hold text or history: they are retired to Salvage, and can be put back. Only an empty, history-less chapter (a mistake) can be deleted.
- 2026-10-21: Experiment items are closed from their chapter (accept/harvest/reject), not from the inbox, so the manuscript and the item can't disagree.
- 2026-10-21: Restore always makes a new world with new ids; it never merges into an existing one. History (revisions) is not exported.
- 2026-10-21: Linking items to chapters is allowed by the data model but has no screen yet; tags on items stay bible entries for v1.
- 2026-10-21: The offline copy covers chapters and their last 30 revisions (sync per world, by time); the rest of a world is read online. The spec's general `change_log` sync wasn't needed for v1.
- 2026-10-21: Text typed in a chapter is kept on the device until the server confirms it; a draft over an older version becomes a conflict revision rather than overwriting anything.
- 2026-10-22: After using v1, the bible becomes a world model: entries with facts and typed relationships, story time, graphs and a writing companion (docs/WORLD-MODEL.md), as phases 6–9.
- 2026-10-22: Chapters are the story clock (facts and relationships hold from/until chapters); in-world dates come later.
- 2026-10-22: Relationship graph and place tree first; uploaded map images with pins later.
- 2026-10-22: New kinds Event and Culture; lore stays in Term.
- 2026-10-22: Research (docs/research/) adds to the world model: evidence on facts and relationships, knows/has met, resolved findings (intentional/later), Drafting vs Revising, getting back in, spell-check skipping names, per-entry "not in this sense". A cuts bin, revision passes, a thread grid, scenes and a corkboard wait for later.
- 2026-10-22: The companion's findings are grouped into five editors (Proofreader, Continuity, Connections, Archivist, Loose ends): tabs that can be switched off, all rule-based, all silent while Drafting.
- 2026-10-22: Phase 6: names are matched in the browser by a port of the server's matcher, from cards kept on the device; the server stays the source for mentions and counts.
- 2026-10-22: Writing preferences are per device (localStorage), not per user: a phone and a desk want different text sizes and card timings.
- 2026-10-22: Selecting a known name shows its card; on Android a long-press selects the word, so that is the phone's long-press without custom touch handling.
- 2026-10-22: Performance budget for typing: press-to-paint within two frames for a 10,000-word chapter and 400 entries, also at 4× slower CPU. Met after remembering each word's misspelling verdict and each paragraph's names, updating the word count when typing pauses, and keeping the chapter sidebar out of per-keystroke re-renders.
- 2026-10-22: Phase 7: a fact with a "from" chapter takes over from earlier facts with the same label, so "status: dead from chapter 9" needs no end date on "alive". Spans are inclusive and must run forwards.
- 2026-10-22: "Is at" is one place per character per chapter; moving within a chapter is a note, not a second row. Derived places (named in the same paragraph) are computed when the grid is read, never stored.
- 2026-10-22: Story order is project order, then chapter order; experiments and retired chapters are outside it, and a span bounded by one simply ignores that bound.
- 2026-10-22: Phase 8: the graph is drawn with d3-force (laid out to rest before drawing, so nothing jiggles) in plain SVG; no graph library. Clusters are Louvain modularity on the links shown, worked out in the browser only when colouring by cluster.
- 2026-10-22: Co-occurrence (named in the same paragraph) is computed from the stored mentions when the graph asks for it (about 50 ms for 400 entries and 10,000 words), not stored per chapter as the spec first planned.
- 2026-10-22: The place tree nests places by a place–place type read as "inside"/"in"/"part of"/"within" (or with the reverse "contains"), so a renamed type still nests if it reads that way.
- 2026-10-22: Phase 9: the companion's findings about the text (Proofreader, Connections, Archivist, Loose ends) are worked out in the browser, live and offline; the Continuity checks run on the server over recorded data. Resolutions are stored per world; they are bookkeeping and are not exported.
- 2026-10-22: Drafting is the default mode: names are coloured, nothing else is marked, and the panel's findings update only at a break (a line of ***, opening a tab, "Update now"). Revising marks findings in the text, worked out again 0.7 s after typing stops.
- 2026-10-22: Not built from the spec's checks: "at two places in the same chapter" (the data allows one "is at" per chapter, so it can't happen) and "knowing too early" (it needs recorded acts, which the model doesn't have). "Have they met?" is the unrecorded-connection finding plus the "has met" type.
- 2026-10-22: Two values of a fact conflict when they start in the same chapter, or when an explicit "until" runs past the next one's "from". An open-ended earlier value is taken over by a later one (that is how "dead from chapter 9" works), so "grey from 1, brown from 4" without an end is a change, not a conflict.
- 2026-10-22: Spelling is the browser's spell-check, with names marked to be skipped; Quarry ships no dictionary. The Proofreader adds doubled words, the same word close together, spacing, likely misspelled names and, optionally, very long sentences.
- 2026-10-22: Leaving a chapter after writing 30 or more words asks once, skippably, for a note for next time (in-app navigation only; closing the tab just keeps the place). The place and the note are per user, and not exported.
- 2026-10-22: Margin notes (%% lines) stay in the chapter and its exported file, but are left out of the word count, the names found in the chapter, and the compiled manuscript.md.
- 2026-10-22: A demo world (The Ninefold Delta, made up) loads from the New world page, through the same code as Restore. It is planted with one of each thing the companion finds, so every panel has something to show. Worlds can now be deleted by their owner, by typing the world's name.
- 2026-10-22: A name or other name that starts with "The" also matches with "the" (and the other way round), on the server and in the browser alike: "The River Wardens" is found in "the flag of the River Wardens".
- 2026-10-24: Added a second round of research (docs/research/05–07: World Anvil, other worldbuilding tools, novel-writing tools) with a summary in the research README. Nothing has been scheduled from it yet.
- 2026-10-24: Phase 10, revising and salvaging, is specced in SPEC.md §11 rather than WORLD-MODEL.md: it is about the manuscript, not the world model.
- 2026-10-24: Renaming an entry keeps the old name as another name by default (server-side, so every place a name is edited behaves the same), with Remove and "Replace in the text…" in the toast. Not offered for a change of capitals, or when the old name belongs to another entry.
- 2026-10-24: Chapter statuses are rows per project (raw, rewritten, polished to start), so a rename reaches every chapter. Summary and status are optional and start empty, also for new chapters.
- 2026-10-24: Cuts are their own table, per project, kept forever: putting one back marks it "put back" instead of removing it. Ctrl+Shift+X cuts the selection; cutting works offline through the outbox. Cuts are not matched for mentions or counted.
- 2026-10-24: Reading one entry's thread is built in the browser from the offline chapters and the name matcher, so it works on the phone without a network. A paragraph is a line; margin notes next to a hit, or naming the entry, are shown.
- 2026-10-24: Word history counts words added and removed per revision (diffed once, when the revision is taken, without margin notes), summed per day in the browser's time zone. A new `start` revision marks text a chapter began with; existing chapters' first revisions count as their start. No streaks, goals or judging colours.
- 2026-10-24: Changes since a checkpoint are chosen by label, remembered per project on the device, and shown only while Revising. "Checkpoint every chapter…" on the project page makes one label cover the book.
- 2026-10-24: Find and replace across chapters is plain text (no patterns), covers current chapters and experiments (never Salvage), checkpoints every chapter it changes first, skips chapters changed since the preview, and has an Undo that restores those checkpoints. It needs the network.
- 2026-10-24: Cuts are never thrown away; a big bin stays usable through Salvage's search.
- 2026-10-24: Chapter statuses are per project, not one set for the whole world: each book can name its own stages.
- 2026-10-24: Reading one entry's thread can take a second entry (With…, or Read together on a relationship) and then shows only the paragraphs or chapters that name both. Two entries at most.
- 2026-10-24: Phase 10 built. A chapter's summary and status are saved without the version check and don't make a new version: they are labels, not text, so setting them on the manuscript list never turns another device's next save into a conflict copy.
- 2026-10-24: "Replace in the text…" after a rename opens find and replace in the project last opened, set to every project when the world has several: names belong to the world.
- 2026-10-24: "Put back where it was" looks for the text before and after the cut together; if that is gone or found twice, for the text on one side alone (at least 12 characters, found once); otherwise the cut goes in at the cursor. Put back from Salvage opens the chapter without a cursor yet, so there it goes at the end, and says so.
- 2026-10-24: Undo right after cutting is the one way a cut is deleted (the text went back). A cut still waiting on the device is simply dropped, and can't be put back from the editor until it has synced.
- 2026-10-24: Word history counts a `start` revision only where the spec says (Restore, the demo, an experiment started as a copy). A chapter made in the app starts empty, so its first words are a day's writing.
- 2026-10-24: The status tally counts the book's current chapters, and gives "none" without words, as in the spec's example. A status removed from the project page asks first when chapters have it, saying how many.
- 2026-10-24: Find and replace's "whole words" means no letter, digit or underscore right before or after the text, so "Varren's" is found and "Varrens" or "Varrenhold" are not. With nothing to replace with, the page is a find whose hits link into their chapters; deleting them is a quieter button that asks first. Experiments follow their project's chapters in the preview.
- 2026-10-24: Changes since a checkpoint list a chapter's labelled revisions except conflict copies, and include margin notes (they are on the page). An older checkpoint fetched once is kept in its own table on the device, so pruning to the last 30 snapshots doesn't drop it.
- 2026-10-24: Not built from §11: a command palette entry for find and replace (Quarry has no command palette yet). Exports also carry each cut's `put_back_to`, so a restore keeps where it went back.
- 2026-10-24: A server answering 502, 503 or 504 (nginx while the app restarts after an update) counts as unreachable, like no network: the app opens from the copy on the device, drafts, offline snapshots and captures wait instead of being dropped, and the sign-in page says the server may be restarting and tries again by itself.
- 2026-10-24: Phase 11, more views of the bible, is specced in SPEC.md §12: views of data Quarry already stores (mentions, relationships, "is at", story order), with two small fields (a gap length per project, a level per entry) and one new relationship type.
- 2026-10-24: The mentions strip is built from the mentions the entry page already gets and the story order on the device: current chapters only, shaded in three steps relative to the entry's busiest chapter, with gaps labelled once they reach the project's gap length (a setting on the project page, 5 chapters to start).
- 2026-10-24: The family tree is a tab of the Graph page, drawn in the browser from the recorded relationships: types read as parent or married count by their label (as the place tree reads "inside"). Kin (siblings, grandparents, cousins, in-laws, step-relations) is worked out when shown, dimmed, never stored, and follows "as of". Words follow the character's gender field (see below).
- 2026-10-24: Someone recorded as their own ancestor is a Continuity check, like a place inside itself.
- 2026-10-24: The Where-everyone-is grid keeps its name and address but takes any kinds or chosen entries as rows; characters stay the default and keep "is at". "Do these share a chapter?" is a column filter worked out in the browser, and claims only "named in the same chapter".
- 2026-10-24: Entries live at two levels, the world (shared) or one project; there is no "several projects", since an entry in two books is a world entry. Names stay unique in the world. New entries made from a chapter start in its project (only when the world has more than one); everything else starts in the world, including every existing entry. The Archivist offers "Share with the world" when a project's entry is named in another project. The level orders bible lists and pickers and filters the grid and the graph, but never matching. Exported as `project:` in the entry's frontmatter; files stay in Bible/<Kind>/. This answers the open question about project-only bible pages.
- 2026-10-24: "Witnessed" (character → event) reaches existing worlds by a data migration, unless a world already has it; Restore keeps an export's own vocabulary. "Pronunciation" (characters and places) is a suggested fact, which reaches every world at once because suggestions are not stored; it shows after the name on the entry page and the card.
- 2026-10-24: Bible lists hide nothing: with a project chosen, entries the book uses (named in its chapters, or in its own bible) come first, and the rest follow dimmed under "Elsewhere", tagged with where they are used ("only in The Salt Road", "not named yet").
- 2026-10-24: No one-time tidy of existing entries into projects: everything so far is test worlds and the demo, so every existing entry simply starts in the world.
- 2026-10-24: Classification (an event's type, a character's pronouns) stays free-form facts: no fixed lists, required fields, forms per kind or category trees. Phase 11 adds ways to make what builds up consistent instead (§12.6).
- 2026-10-24: Fact labels and values are read per kind (an event's type and a place's type are different domains) and match whatever the capitals. Value suggestions work like label suggestions: the values used for that label on the same kind, most used first, on the entry page only.
- 2026-10-24: A Labels page renames and merges labels (within a kind, optionally everywhere) and values (within a label and kind) across the world, with Undo. Labels and values aren't stored on their own; a small `fact_labels` table holds only the per-label switches.
- 2026-10-24: A label can be marked as holding several values (per kind). Then values that hold at once are not a conflict, "as of" keeps them all, and they show as chips. This amends the phase 9 conflict rule, which still applies to every other label.
- 2026-10-24: "Type" (events) and "pronouns" (characters) join the suggested facts, reaching every world at once. A label on three or more entries of a kind is "common": listed first and offered as "Add:" on entries without it, never as a warning.
- 2026-10-24: A missing label is an Archivist finding only for labels marked "expected" for a kind on the Labels page; the page points out candidates but never marks one by itself. Resolved per entry ("add it" or "not for this one") through the existing resolutions; never inbox items.
- 2026-10-24: When a label reaches three entries of a kind, the entry page asks once, inline and without taking focus, whether to expect it on every entry of that kind ("Expect it" / "Not now"). Asked once per label and kind, recorded on the server when shown, so ignoring it counts as "Not now"; the Labels page stays the place to change it. Never in the editor.
- 2026-10-24: Gender is a structured field on characters, not a fact: female, male, nonbinary or not set (a nullable column with a CHECK), optional, not time-bounded, edited on the entry page, exported as `gender:` and restored. It is the one exception to free-form classification, because the family tree needs it to pick its words and shouldn't interpret free text. Existing characters start not set; nothing is inferred from facts. A "gender" fact is no longer suggested; "pronouns" stays, for nuance.
- 2026-10-24: Kin words: female and male give mother/father, daughter/son, sister/brother, aunt/uncle, niece/nephew, grandmother/grandfather and so on; nonbinary and not set give one general word per relation: parent, child, sibling, half-sibling, grandparent, grandchild, parent's sibling, sibling's child, parent-in-law, child-in-law, sibling-in-law, step-parent, stepchild, spouse. "Parent's sibling" and "sibling's child" over "pibling"/"nibling" (clearer) or "aunt or uncle" (still gendered). Cousin, relative, ancestor and descendant are the same for everyone.
- 2026-10-24: A character's card shows the gender only when set, as one small word after the name.
- 2026-10-24: Phase 11 built. The cards on the device now carry every canon fact of an entry (they were capped at 12), the labels of all its facts whatever their standing, its level and its gender, plus the world's label switches, so the bible's tables, "as of" with several values, "Add:" and the expected-label findings work from them offline.
- 2026-10-24: The mentions strip numbers chapters in story order across the projects (one book: its chapter numbers), and names the project on a cell's tip when there are several. "Longest gap" names only a real gap (a run that reaches the gap length); a run shorter than that isn't called a gap anywhere. The gap length is set in the project's Edit form.
- 2026-10-24: The family tree also reads the mirror of a parent type (a label ending in "son of" / "daughter of" / "child of", or a reverse ending in "parent of" / "father of" / "mother of", makes B the parent), and "brother of" / "sister of" as recorded siblings. "Great-" goes up to four (great-great-great-great-grandparent); further is ancestor or descendant. In the tree a recorded link of a plain type (parent of, married to, sibling of) gets the gendered word like kin worked out ("mother", "husband"); one of another type keeps its own label ("adoptive parent of"). The entry page's recorded relationships keep their labels as always. The † reads a "status" fact as the Continuity check does (dead, deceased, died, killed, slain, murdered).
- 2026-10-24: Someone recorded as their own ancestor is checked over canon relationships only, as a place inside itself is.
- 2026-10-24: "A project chosen in the switcher" for the bible lists is a Book picker on the bible pages, kept in the address (?book=) and starting from the project last opened on the device. The kind tables also sort by a clicked column, which the spec took to exist already.
- 2026-10-24: A world with one project keeps every entry in the world on the server too: a level sent for an entry there is ignored. Export writes `gender:` whenever it is set (also for an entry whose kind changed away from character, which keeps the value), so Restore loses nothing.
- 2026-10-24: The "witnessed" migration counts a type with "*" on a side as joining characters and events. It goes after the world's last character → event type, or at the end of the list when there is none.
- 2026-10-24: Label counts are entries, not facts, and every standing counts for labels, values, suggestions and renames; the bible's tables filter and group by canon facts, as the cards carry them. The Labels page points out a candidate for "expected" when at least three quarters of a kind's entries (and at least four) have the label.
- 2026-10-24: The "expect it?" question is asked when adding a fact brings its label to three or more entries of the kind, not exactly three, so a world whose labels were already common gets the question once, at their next use. Still once per label and kind, marked asked in the same save.
- 2026-10-24: On the entry page a fact's value edits in place, so filtering the kind's table by it is a small ≡ link beside the value. A label with several values shows its values as chips in one row, each with its own span and remove. "Add it" opens the entry with the label filled in and the value field focused.
- 2026-10-24: Where everyone is keeps its choice (kinds, added and hidden rows, ticks, the share filter, the project) in the address, and the last one per world in the device's local storage; a page opened without a choice in the address starts from it. A hidden row that was added by hand stays in the address, so it can be shown again.
- 2026-10-24: "Share with the world" is keyed per entry and per book it was named in: "Intentional" silences it for that book only. "Both are true (several values)" is on the conflict both in the editor's Continuity tab and on the Checks page.
- 2026-10-25: Phase 12, time, history and kinds of being, is specced in SPEC.md §13, with restructuring added (§13.6). Built as three phases: 12a (time on one axis and restructuring), 12b (history, the Timeline, recording events from the prose, ages) and 12c (Species, roles in groups, more starting vocabulary).
- 2026-10-25: In-world time is one sortable number (decimals and below zero allowed) with an optional free label, an "about" tick and an optional end, in a unit the world names (years by default). No calendar engine: no months, moons or date arithmetic. This replaces "in-world dates come later".
- 2026-10-25: Chapters have two orders: told (book order, as before) and happened (by the time a chapter is set, then book order). A chapter with no time carries on from the last chapter before it that has one and isn't marked as a tale or flashback; a tale or flashback with no time is set sometime before the story, and the time checks skip it. With no times and no marks, happened order is book order and nothing changes.
- 2026-10-25: Told order is the reader's (manuscript, mentions strip and gaps, first appearance, thread reading, find and replace); happened order is the world's (facts, relationships, as of, cards, the family tree, the Continuity checks, ages). "Born" and "arrives" are checked on the clock, "introduced" and "first appears" in book order. This amends the phase 7 rule that story order alone bounds spans.
- 2026-10-25: A bound on a fact or relationship is a chapter or an in-world time, never both, so history before the book can be recorded. A chapter with no set time ignores time bounds.
- 2026-10-25: Events get a "when": a chapter they happen in (following its time) or a time. Eras are events whose type is "era", drawn as bands; History is the Timeline cut off where the book begins. Neither has a table of its own.
- 2026-10-25: Record as event (selection strip) keeps the passage as a telling of the event, suggests participants only from the names found in the selection, and may leave the time empty. Names inside a telling's passage don't count for "named after being recorded dead".
- 2026-10-25: Species is a ninth kind, called Species (Thomas), shown as a word after a character's name, a column on the characters table and a graph colouring. Existing worlds get its starting types by migration; an export with no type touching species gets them on Restore, since the kind didn't exist when it was made.
- 2026-10-25: A role is a field on relationships of types marked "has a role" (member of, leads, serves and works at to start), time-bounded with the relationship. "One at a time" is a switch per role across the world, off by default; two canon holders of it in the same group at the same time, a handover aside, is a Continuity finding.
- 2026-10-25: "Ancestor of" is drawn in the family tree as a long dashed link labelled with the gap in years when both births are known; no kin beyond ancestor and descendant is worked out across it. Starting values (a place's type, an event's type, a species' form) are a list in the code, like suggested facts, offered after the values in use.
- 2026-10-25: A species is described with suggested facts and value suggestions, not fields: form, sapient, size, lifespan, habitat, diet and reproduction (plus appearance, pronunciation, name meaning), each of which can be marked expected for Species. A species attribute becomes a structured field only once something reads it, as gender did.
- 2026-10-25: A fact or relationship can be bounded by a chapter or by an in-world time on each side, and the two may be mixed; both sit on the one clock (a "from" time just before the chapters set at it, an "until" just after). Anchoring to time is what survives restructuring: "dead from year 312" outlives any reshuffle, "dead from chapter 9" does not.
- 2026-10-25: An experiment can replace a range of current chapters with several drafts, written beside the book without changing it. Accepting puts the drafts in at the range's place and retires the range; harvesting or rejecting retires the drafts. A single-chapter experiment is the range of one; chapters.replaces_id becomes experiment_ranges.
- 2026-10-25: Retiring chapters (one, several, or by closing an experiment) first lists everything tied to them (bounds, evidence, "is at", events and tellings, items, the note for next time) with keep (re-anchor to the chapter's set time, or hold throughout without one), move (preselected where likely), mark as planned, or drop, per row or in bulk, with Undo. Nothing is changed silently. This replaces the 2026-10-22 rule that a bound on a retired chapter is ignored; bounds already on retired chapters are read as before until reviewed from Salvage, and the migration changes nothing.
- 2026-10-25: Evidence pointing into a retired chapter is an Archivist finding, resolved by re-anchoring (chapters where the passage is found come first) or dismissing.
- 2026-10-25: Thomas's answers to the phase 12 questions follow. A tale or flashback with no time keeps the default: it is set before the story, and the time checks skip it.
- 2026-10-25: "Outside time" is a third kind of time, beside a time and not set, for planes beyond the world, an afterlife, visions, dreams, myths and prophecies. Chapters, events, facts and relationships can be outside time. It is never placed on the axis, never carried on to the next chapter, never compared by Continuity or ages, always shown under "as of" (labelled outside time, never taking over or taken over), and has its own strip on the Timeline. Exported as `time: outside` and `outside_time: true`.
- 2026-10-25: Eras stay events of the type "era" (a page, mentions and relationships for free), with a colour from a palette of eight that works in both themes. A time's era is the innermost era holding it, and every time shown in the app carries its era's tint and name ("year 312 · the Lamp Years").
- 2026-10-25: The Timeline is to scale with empty stretches folded, with zoom and pan, semantic zoom (clusters with counts further out), collapsible lanes per character, faction or era, the editor's hover cards, click-through, brushing a span to filter the rest, an overview strip, and a budget of 60 frames a second with 3,000 items. Plain SVG, no library.
- 2026-10-25: "One at a time" is a switch per role across the world (Thomas: yes, as specced).
- 2026-10-25: A computed age that disagrees with an "age" fact by more than one unit (more with "about") is a Continuity finding; an age value that doesn't start with a number is never compared.
- 2026-10-25: "Keep" in the review, for a chapter with no time, makes the bound go: the row holds throughout on that side (Thomas: as specced).
- 2026-10-25: An experiment may only add chapters: a range of none, placed before or after a chapter (a new prologue). Accepting inserts the drafts and retires nothing.
- 2026-10-25: Build order 12c, then 12a, then 12b, then 12d (the chart), by one build session as stacked pull requests (12c off master, 12a off 12c, 12b off 12a, 12d off 12b).
