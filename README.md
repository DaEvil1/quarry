# Quarry

A writing environment for long fiction set in shared worlds: capture sparks on the phone, triage them at the desk, keep a living bible of the world, and write the manuscript in a fast, dark editor.

Quarry does bookkeeping only. It never generates, suggests or critiques story content.

**Status:** v1: all five phases built. Sign in with a passkey; create worlds and their projects; capture sparks, questions and tasks on the phone (also offline, and by sharing from other apps); triage them at the desk with the keyboard; keep a decisions log and a journal; keep a bible of the world, see where every name is mentioned, and fix misspelled names in one click; write chapters in a distraction-free editor with history, experiments and salvage; export a world as Markdown and restore it; and on the phone, open and write chapters and browse and restore their history with no network. Phases 6–9 of the world model ([docs/WORLD-MODEL.md](docs/WORLD-MODEL.md)) are in: bible entries carry a one-liner, facts and relationships that read both ways; each kind has its own table; and the editor colours names by kind as they are typed, shows a name's card when you rest on it (never while typing), and turns a selection into a new entry or links names from their card. Chapters are the story's clock: facts and relationships hold from/until a chapter, entry pages and cards read *as of* a chapter, and a grid shows where everyone is per chapter, recorded or derived from the text. A relationship graph (filters, as of a chapter, centred on someone with their first- and second-order links, clusters) and a place tree show the world at a glance, and the manuscript lists each chapter's cast. A writing companion beside the editor groups rule-based findings into five editors (Proofreader, Continuity, Connections, Archivist, Loose ends), quiet while Drafting and marked in the text while Revising, with Alt+Enter for quick fixes, F8 for a classifying pass, Ctrl+Shift+S to stash a thought, margin notes, a note for next time, and a Checks page for the whole world. Phase 10, revising and salvaging ([docs/SPEC.md](docs/SPEC.md) §11), is in too: a rename keeps the old name as another name; chapters have a one-line summary and a status (each project names its own stages); Ctrl+Shift+X cuts a passage to the project's bin (also offline), from where it can be put back; an entry's page reads every paragraph that names it, alone or together with a second entry, on the phone with no network too; the project page shows the words written and cut each day; while Revising the editor tints what changed since a labelled checkpoint; and find and replace works across chapters with a preview, a checkpoint per chapter and Undo. Phase 11, more views of the bible ([docs/SPEC.md](docs/SPEC.md) §12), adds a mentions strip on each entry page (where in the story it is named, and the gaps); a family tree with kin worked out (siblings, cousins, in-laws, step-relations), worded by a character's gender field; the Where-everyone-is grid for any entries, with "do these share a chapter?"; entries that live in one book's bible or the world's; the *witnessed* relationship and a *pronunciation* fact; and labels and values: value suggestions, filtering and grouping a kind's table by a value, a Labels page to rename and merge them (with Undo), labels that hold several values, and labels expected on every entry, asked once at the third use and found by the Archivist. Phase 12 ([docs/SPEC.md](docs/SPEC.md) §13) adds Species as a ninth kind (a word after a character's name, a column on the characters table, a graph colouring), roles in groups with a "one at a time" check, and more starting vocabulary: *possesses*, *ancestor of* (a long dashed link in the family tree), directions between places, *name meaning*, and starting values for a place's or an event's type and a species' form. Chapters can be set in in-world time (a number in the world's unit, or outside time) and marked as a tale or a flashback, so facts, relationships, *as of* and the Checks follow when things happen rather than the order they are told; facts can be bounded by a time; experiments can replace a run of chapters with several drafts, or only add some; and nothing leaves the book without a review of what is tied to it, with Undo. Events have a *when* (a chapter, a time, or outside time) and eras are events in a colour of their own, so every time shown carries its era; a Timeline page lays the events, the book, births, deaths and movements on one axis, to scale with empty stretches folded, with lanes per character, faction or era, clusters that split as you zoom in, brushing a span, an overview strip, a told view and History; a selected passage can be recorded as an event (kept as a telling of it) and births and deaths set from it; and ages are worked out from birth times. A chart draws the places as a schematic map from what is recorded: lands as hexagons holding their towns, water around its islands, borders touching, roads and sea routes as lines, north up, who controls each place and who is there as of a chapter or a time; places dragged on it stay pinned, and beside the Timeline it follows a brushed span and lists what changed across it. None of it writes or suggests story content. To see it all without writing anything, load the demo world from the New world page. See [docs/SPEC.md](docs/SPEC.md) for the design and the phases, and [docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md) for the decisions behind it.
